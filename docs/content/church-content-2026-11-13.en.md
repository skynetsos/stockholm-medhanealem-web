# About Our Church

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, one God. Amen.

## Debre Selam Medhanealem Church, Archiepiscopal See of the Diocese of Sweden and Scandinavia of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church

### Stockholm

Before we look at the founding of Stockholm's Debre Selam Medhanealem Church, the archiepiscopal see of the Diocese of Sweden and Scandinavia of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, and at the spiritual and social services carried out within it, we will briefly explain, by way of introduction, what the word "church" means.

# What Does "Church" Mean?

"Church" (bete kristiyan) means "the house of the Christian", and it carries two main meanings. The first is the gathering, or unity, of the faithful. The second refers to the church building, built according to the canons of the Church, whether in an ancient or a modern style, whether of gold and silver or of mud and straw, whether in the city or in the countryside, as circumstances allow, where the worship of God is performed and the Body and Blood are given to the faithful.

In other words, since God does not withdraw His help from the place where He is worshipped, it is called the house of God and the house of prayer. "For now I have chosen and sanctified this house, that My name may be there forever; and My eyes and My heart will be there always" (2 Chronicles 7:16). Likewise in the New Testament: "Where two or three are gathered together in My name, there I am in the midst of them" (Matthew 18:20).

Because Ethiopia, in the time of the Old Testament, was a country that kept the Law according to the law God gave to Moses and offered the sacrifices of the Law, it was no stranger to the Church founded by the blood of Christ in the time of the New Testament. The rest of the world, which had long persisted in the worship of idols, struggled to believe and to abandon its habits of worship even when the apostles were sent to it, and it killed the apostles who taught the word of life.

Ethiopia, however, went out herself and received the Christian faith through the eunuch of the queen called Candace. In the third century she brought the Christian faith she had received to completeness in its order, spread it through baptism, founded many monasteries, built many churches, and raised up millions of faithful; and she has lived, and continues to live, giving spiritual and social service.

The Church, too, has done great work so that the holy land of Ethiopia might have the light of knowledge, be the only African country with its own alphabet and its own calendar, and be the owner of art, literature, architecture, scholarship and history.

# A Short History of Debre Selam Medhanealem Archiepiscopal Cathedral, Stockholm

In accordance with the decision of the Holy Synod, chaired by His Holiness Abune Merkorios, Patriarch and Archbishop of Ethiopia, in January 1989 (European calendar) His Grace Abune Elias, at that time Archbishop of the dioceses of Djibouti, Kenya, Germany and Greece, came bringing the Kale Awadi, the statute by which the church is governed. After informing S.S.T. (the government office in Sweden that grants subsidies to the various faith communities living in the country) and the relevant institutions, and putting everything in order, he returned to Ethiopia.

Because bringing the tabot and blessing it required a priest who would receive the tabot and serve permanently, the church's founding committee followed up on the visa permit to be issued by Swedish immigration. When the permit was delayed, His Grace returned to Sweden and went in person to the immigration office in Norrköping, explaining not only its necessity but also its urgency. He secured the visa and sent it to the priest, and so the first priest, Liqe Kahenat Mezemir Biru, was able to come bringing the holy tabot on 20 October 1989 (European calendar).

His Grace Abune Elias, together with the people, received it, blessed the tabot and placed it in a small Swedish church in Aspudden, after which His Grace returned to Ethiopia. The holy tabot remained in that church for five months and was then moved to Klara kyrka in April of the same year.

As Saint Paul said, “Embizha himamomu fedfede fishahomu”: out of the steadfastness of their affliction their glory abounded (2 Corinthians 8:2). So too, the faithful who have been with the church from the beginning say that, when we look at the stage it has now reached, it has passed through many trials.

Under the guidance of His Grace, the registration under number 802013–8940, held since 1988 (European calendar) in the name of an association, was brought in 2002 to trossamfund (registered faith community) status, the number that grants the church recognition as a religious institution, and the church was registered under the name “Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church”. In this way he enabled us to have our own church, in which we now worship.

With God as our helper, in order to move out of this hall-like house of prayer and build a church building that bears the form and order of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, we have taken over the football ground next to our church, where we celebrate Demera Meskel every year. Having passed through all the stages, we are now only awaiting the building permit to begin the work, that is, the construction.

However, the requirement that the government office issuing building permits wants to see first is proof that we have the money with which to build the church building. We can meet this requirement only when we gain up to one thousand members paying monthly by autogiro (direct debit), as the bank has set for us, or when we can show fifty percent of the building cost in cash. We are therefore calling on all the Christian faithful to rally for their faith and their history, so that they may hand on this living and everlasting work to their children born in a foreign land.

Since it is Medhane Alem Himself who prepared His own sanctuary and the home of each one of us, we say: let us believe that to be called to a holy work is to be chosen, and let us not miss this opportunity to receive great blessing.

The main purpose for which our church was founded is to help Ethiopians who have come to or taken refuge in this country for various reasons, and those of Ethiopian descent born in the diaspora, to know that, beginning with the rite of baptism (christening), they have their own faith and Christian order through which they meet their Creator. Beyond this, because they also find their culture and their language in the church, it plays a key role in affirming their identity.

# Christening (Baptism)

After humankind abandoned the law it had been commanded to keep, became estranged from its Creator and was driven out of Paradise, it was condemned to death of the body and death of the soul, to descent into the grave and descent into Sheol, and lived a life of suffering and affliction, held in bondage by the devil, who had not created it. God, grieved by the suffering of humankind, according to the promise of salvation He had made, took our nature as His own, became man, and restored humankind to its former glory.

Accordingly, He affirmed that humankind cannot attain the gift of salvation that has been given to it without baptism, saying: “Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God.” (John 3:5)

Therefore, Christening is the rite in which the great mystery is accomplished through which we receive adoption as children (as He is called Christ, we are called Christians; as He is called the Son, we are called children), are called by His name, are clothed with His glory, inherit His kingdom, and live forever.

# Marriage

It is the Most High God who established marriage. Having created our father Adam, He placed him in the Garden of Eden. But after seeing that he was alone, and knowing that he needed a helper, He took one bone from his body and created Eve for him as a wife. “And the Lord God said: It is not good that man should be alone; let us make for him a helper suitable for him.” (Genesis 2:18)

Marriage, which God established in this way, was also blessed and sanctified in later times by God the Son, when He put on flesh and was revealed on earth, by being present at a wedding feast.

Our Church, too, founding herself on this holy law of her Creator, solemnises marriage and joins couples together through the rite of Teklil (crowning) and Holy Communion.

## The Purposes of Marriage

1. **For mutual help:** For it is written, “Let us make for him a helper suitable for him.” (Genesis 2:18)

2. **To bring forth offspring:** For it is written, “Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth.” (Genesis 1:28)

3. **To be kept from fornication:** “Because of the danger of fornication, let each man have his own wife, and let each woman have her own husband.” (1 Corinthians 7:2)

# Offerings (Mebae)

**Offering (mebae) means:** the gift that each person brings to God with his own hand, as he has purposed in his heart. This is a divine commandment, for God commanded Moses, saying: “Speak to the children of Israel, that they bring Me a gift; from every man whose heart moves him to give it to Me with his own hand, receive the offering.” (Exodus 25:2) Since the word mebae means gift, it includes the firstfruits, the firstborn, the tithe and the vow.

**Firstfruits (qedamyat) means:** since the word means “those that ripen first”, it refers to the grain harvest (the crops). It shows that, for believers who live by farming, giving to God the first of the crop that ripens first is a divine commandment and a Christian duty.

**Firstborn (bekurat) means:** whether of humans or of animals, that which comes first, or that which opens its mother’s womb and comes forth, the one born first. It means giving or offering to God, beginning with a person giving his first or firstborn child to become a servant of God, and also the firstborn of his cattle, his sheep, his goats and the like.

**Tithe (asrat) means:** one out of ten. It means that the merchant from his profit and the salaried worker from his salary takes one tenth, saying, “This belongs to God, not to me,” and gives it to the Church. These gifts called the firstfruits, the firstborn and the tithe are commandments that every Christian ought to fulfil. If a person does not fulfil these commandments, he is said to have robbed God, as the prophet Malachi said: “Will a man rob God? Yet you have robbed Me. And you said, ‘In what have we robbed You?’ In tithes and in firstborn offerings.” (Malachi 3:8–9)

Our Lord, too, confirmed this commandment in the Gospel, saying, “Render to God the things that are God’s.” (Matthew 22:21)

It is only that a person does not take it to heart and understand it, but he has nothing that is truly his own. Since even his moving in life belongs to God, if he gives back to God from what God has given him, he receives blessing through it.

**The vow (selet),** however, differs from the commandments we have mentioned above. A vow means fulfilling the promise that a person, for various reasons, has made in speaking with God. “If a man makes a vow to the Lord, or binds himself by an oath, he shall not break his word; he shall do according to all that has come out of his mouth.” (Numbers 30:2) **Almsgiving (mitswat),** on the other hand, means a person giving to God, from what he has, what his heart has purposed.

# Fitihat (Prayer for the Departed)

Humankind is commanded to live its earthly life pleasing the Lord its God: standing firm in the faith, engaged in good works, keeping far from sin and preserving purity. If a person lives his earthly life keeping God’s law and fulfilling His commandments, then, because the good works he did on earth follow him and earn him a heavenly reward, he will dwell in Paradise until the Second Coming, and afterwards in the Kingdom of Heaven with His holy angels.

However, even though every person lives in the faith, because of the weak nature of the flesh, it is the Creator alone who knows whether a person has done only perfect works of righteousness and been called to God. Therefore, whenever any Christian dies, the prayer of absolution for the departed (fitihat) is performed.

The prayer of absolution is performed for two reasons. The first is that, as St Yared said, “Wesib’e yikebir emkulu fitret” (“Man is honoured above all creation”), man alone is honoured above all creation and created in the likeness of his Creator; therefore, when he takes leave of this world and goes to his Creator, it is fitting that he be sent off with praise. The second is that, as the fathers say, “Man is never without error, nor iron without rust”; as we mentioned above, if there is anything by which he grieved God through the nature of the flesh he wore, it is so that God may forgive him. For it is written, “Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find.” (Matthew 7:7)

# Annual Major Feasts

## a) The Feast of St John, New Year (Enkutatash)

As its name indicates, this is a great feast in our Church. As at each of the major feasts, the praise of God prepared to suit the day is offered. Beyond that, it is the feast at which the Computus of Demetrius, called Bahire Hasab (Hasabe Bahir), is explained and analysed: the reckoning by which the year is measured by the sun and the moon (by day and by night), and by which the annual feasts and fasts derived by calculation are proclaimed. This reckoning speaks of our Church as a great and ancient church, never parted from the grace of the Holy Spirit, whose knowledge and spiritual wealth, deep and beyond the searching of the human mind, is found in no other religious body; and it bears witness that our country Ethiopia is the only historic nation with its own alphabet and calendar.

## b) Demera and Meskel (Finding of the True Cross):

As we learn each year, this feast marks the finding of the Cross, and it is one of the feasts that our Church has had recognised (registered) as world heritage.

We too celebrate this great feast with a vibrant celebration attended by many faithful. We build the Demera on the football pitch near our church, the ground given to us to build a church on; with the blessing and words of counsel of His Grace our father, the priests wear the vestments befitting the honour of the Cross, and the celebration is accompanied by the hymns of the Sunday School choir.

## c) Tikimt Medhane Alem:

As our parish bears the name of Medhane Alem, this is the greatest of our parish's annual feasts. Many faithful attend from neighbouring countries and from various cities in Sweden, especially from Gothenburg St Gabriel; three days of seb'ate wongel (preaching) gatherings are held; the Wazema (eve) and Mahlet (night hymns) are sung standing, and the tabot (ark) comes out from its throne of glory as the patronal feast is celebrated.

## d) Christmas (Lidet / Genna):

The Feast of the Nativity is a distinctive feast of our Church, on which the reconciliation of humanity with the Creator began, and on which humans and angels together offered praise. As is customary, the Mahlet is sung at its appointed hour by the scholars of our parish, and after the Kidase (Divine Liturgy) is completed, the feast concludes with the blessing and words of counsel of His Grace.

## e) Timket (Epiphany) and Kana Zegelila:

The feasts of Timket and Kana Zegelila are known to be major feasts of our Church. Although we have not yet begun to bring the tabots out from their thrones of glory and celebrate outside the church, as is done in our country Ethiopia, we nevertheless celebrate the Feast of Timket with the Kidase and by performing the rite of the blessing of the waters.

For the Feast of Kana Zegelila, while the Kidase and the spiritual services are kept, the St Michael Association, one of the tsiwa mahber (fellowship associations) established in our parish, uses the Saturday and Sunday nearest the feast to invite a preacher and a singer of mezmur (hymns) from Ethiopia and other countries and to organise a three-day preaching gathering. Beyond feeding the faithful with the Word of God, they also prepare, alongside it, various fundraising activities that do not conflict with the order of the Church, and raise substantial funds to help build the church building.

## f) Megabit Medhane Alem:

Like the feast in Tikimt, Megabit Medhane Alem is celebrated with great splendour: the Wazema and Mahlet are sung standing, the tabot comes out from its throne of glory, and there is teaching of the Gospel and hymns by the Sunday School choir.

## g) Megabit, the Feast of the Incarnation (Annunciation):

Because it is the day on which our Lord became man, the day on which, to put on flesh and save the world, the Word in person came to dwell in the womb of the Virgin Mary at the annunciation of St Gabriel, the Kidase of this feast, one of the nine major feasts of our Lord, is celebrated in our parish.

## h) Hosanna (Palm Sunday), Holy Week, Maundy Thursday (washing of the feet), the Crucifixion and Easter (Fasika)

As is done in the great monasteries and churches, the praise of God appointed for each day and each hour, both the readings and the prostrations, is carried out at its hour.

## i) Ginbot Lideta (Nativity of St Mary):

On her day we celebrate her Kidase. Through the preparations of the St Mary tsiwa mahber, on the Sunday that falls before or after her day, the Wazema and Mahlet are sung standing by the scholars of our parish and the tabot comes out from its throne of glory. When possible a singer of mezmur and a preacher are invited; when not, the feast is celebrated with great splendour through teaching of the Gospel given by the scholars of our parish and the hymns of the Sunday School choir.

## j) Ascension (Erget):

The Feast of the Ascension is one of the nine major feasts of our Lord, and it is celebrated with the Kidase and with teaching on the greatness of the feast.

## k) Sene St Michael:

Coordinated by the St Michael tsiwa mahber, it is celebrated every year in our parish with great splendour, beginning from the eve with the Mahlet sung standing and the tabot brought out from its throne of glory.

Indeed, what makes Sene St Michael distinctive in our parish is this:

First, because it is summer and warm in the country where we live, we are able to carry the tabot outside the church and process around the church, enthroning it as is done back home; and

Second, because the day the Ethiopian flag was planted at the door of our church falls on the day of Sene St Michael, we also commemorate "Flag Day" together.

This has helped children born in this country to come to know Ethiopia when they see the flag.

## l) The Fast of Filseta (Assumption of St Mary):

In the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, the service of the Fast of Filseta of St Mary is offered for a full twenty-four hours, like the angels.

In our parish too, from Sibhate Fiqur to Teamir (the Miracles of Mary), and from the Kidase to the evening supplication (Serk Mihila), it is carried out as is done in the great monasteries and churches.

In our parish, the Divine Liturgy is celebrated every month for Medhane Alem, Our Lady and Saint Michael. If the season is not a fast and the liturgy is a morning liturgy, a teaching on the Gospel is also given.

# Weekly Services

## a) Every Sunday (the Lord's day)

Every Sunday, the Lord's day, as is done in the great churches, the Mezmure Yared (the hymns of St Yared) is sung through the night at our church; after we have recited the night's Teamire Maryam and Teamire Iyesus (the Miracles of Mary and of Jesus), we perform baptisms and proceed to the Kidan (the Covenant prayer) and the Kidase (Divine Liturgy), after which the teaching of the Gospel is given in full.

Since we have mentioned the word mezmur (hymn), one thing everyone ought to know is that nowadays the name is seen to have changed, and it is widely used for the songs of singers who release and sell hymn cassettes and for the singing performed together by Sunday school choirs.

But what is truly called mezmur is the composition of St Yared, sung every week through Saturday night into Sunday, which is the foundation of the index (gitsawe) for the readings read in the midst of the Kidase, for the Gospel that is read and for the Kidase that is celebrated; it is unrivalled in its mystery and in the sweetness of its melody, and the scholars have laboured over it.

A priest is able to lead the mezmur only after labouring long in study. This means that he must thoroughly know at least the studies called Tsome Digua and Me'eraf, Zimare and Mewase'it, Qine and Aquaquam. Only after this is it said of someone that he is a merigeta (master of hymnody) who can lead the mezmur on his own, that is, that he needs no assistant because he is complete in his knowledge. It should therefore be considered that to hear the mezmur, sung after such labour, called "so-and-so's mezmur" by people who have not even counted the letters of the alphabet ("ha ge'ez, hu ka'ib"), let alone learned to read the Weddase Maryam, robs the labour of the scholars of its worth.

## b) Every Wednesday and Friday

Every Wednesday and Friday, the prayer of supplication and the teaching of the Gospel are held from 18:00, Swedish time.

## c) Every Saturday

Every Saturday, with the consent and at the wish of their parents, we teach children studying for the diaconate the alphabet and reading, the oral lessons and the service of the diaconate. Through the results of this Saturday diaconate programme, ordination to the diaconate has twice been conferred at our church, and we are now teaching a third group.

On the other hand, it is known that children born in a foreign land lose their identity when they grow up without knowing their parents' language and without learning the alphabet of their homeland. To spare itself this reproach of history, our church has set up a committee and, having requested a hall from the municipality at Rågsved centrum, teaches Amharic there.

## d) Saturday afternoons

Also on Saturday afternoons, we make home and hospital visits to sprinkle holy water on those who have given birth, to comfort those who mourn, to visit the sick and to reconcile those who have quarrelled.

## e) Monday to Friday, 8–12 in the morning

From Monday to Friday, from 8 to 12 in the morning, we do the great work of saving lives: we counsel people who are troubled for various reasons with the Word of God, so that they are not exposed to serious health problems, encouraging them to give their worries to God and to pray placing their hope in God alone, and helping them to become calm.

When, failing to heed the divine word "What God has joined together, let no man separate", disagreements arise between spouses for various reasons, we help them consider the harm this brings upon themselves and their children, counsel them from the Holy Bible and the teaching of the order of the Church so that they may return to their former love and unity, and arrange the conditions for them to be reconciled.

In this way we have counselled even our sisters who had left their homes, scattered their children and gone to the social services, so that they would speak face to face, through discussion acknowledge and correct their mistakes, and return to their former lives.

## f) Confession and Holy Communion

Faithful who are prepared to make their confession and receive Holy Communion meet with their confession father (their father of the soul), learn about the mystery of confession and how it is carried out and about the mystery of Holy Communion and how it is carried out, and, having learned, make their confession, on the days from Monday to Friday, at a time appointed for them.

# About Our Church

We feel spiritual pride in declaring that the Debre Selam Medhanealem Archiepiscopal Cathedral (Menbere Papasat) is a church filled with love and peace, and an example worthy of its standing as an archiepiscopal see for other churches. This is thanks to the guidance of Abune Elias, the long-serving and senior Archbishop of Sweden and Scandinavia; to two regular priests; to the voluntary service of three priests, among them Melake Hiwot Kesis Getu, and five deacons; and to the service given in harmony and cooperation by the members of the parish council and the building committee, who are full of diligence and sincerity.

The growing number of faithful who register as members, and of children baptized each time the Kidase is celebrated, is sufficient evidence of this.

May God send peace to Ethiopia.
